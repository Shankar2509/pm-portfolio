## The problem

Content discovery was passive. A catalogue, a few algorithmic rows, and no reason for a
user to come back on a day when nothing new had dropped.

<!-- WRITE THIS: How did you notice? What did the data show, or what did you observe?

## The insight

Discovery is social. A recommendation from a person outperforms a row of thumbnails — and
users will produce those recommendations for free if you give them somewhere to put them.

<!-- WRITE THIS: Where did this come from? Something you saw elsewhere? An observation
     about how people actually talk about shows? Say it in your own words. -->

## What I built

Public watchlists. Users curate lists of titles, share them, and other users can like and
comment on them.

## Outcome, honestly

The watchlist shipped in a release alongside EPG improvements and HLS streaming
optimizations. Retention across that release rose 12%.

**I can't tell you how much of that was the watchlist.** The three changes weren't
instrumented independently, so the honest answer is that they moved together and I don't
know the split.
